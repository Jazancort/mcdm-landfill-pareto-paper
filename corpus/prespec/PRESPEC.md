## 1. Purpose

Demonstrate that the amplitude identity (Proposition 4) and the TV decomposition
(Propositions 4–5) operate on published real-world decision matrices outside the
spatial geometry of the Fogo candidate set.

Primary deliverable: table of (TV, m_eff, ||delta||_2, TV_B, TV_W) for each
corpus instance under the three standard normalisations. This is deterministic
and requires no Monte Carlo. It answers §8.7: "the joint distribution of
(TV, m_eff) in applied matrices is unknown."

Secondary (conditional on WS2): the ordering Q2(mm) ≥ Q2(vec) in instances
with coupling constraints. The corpus alone cannot deliver this — see §6.

## 2. Inclusion Criteria (pre-declared; prospective for matrices not yet extracted)

A matrix enters the corpus if and only if ALL of the following hold:

1. **Published:** appeared in a peer-reviewed article or conference proceedings.
2. **Numerical:** the decision matrix X (m × n) is reported with exact values.
3. **Weights declared:** w_j are explicitly stated (any method; equal is fine).
4. **Directions declared:** benefit/cost direction stated or unambiguously inferable.
5. **Minimum size:** n ≥ 3 AND m ≥ 8 (see §5).
6. **Raw matrix available:** not pre-normalised or pre-weighted.
7. **Hierarchy published:** a criterion hierarchy (AHP tree, BWM structure, or
   explicit grouping into 2–5 themes) is published in the source article.
   Instances without a published hierarchy enter the amplitude table only
   and are excluded from any TV_W analysis.

Matrices generated by simulation do not qualify. Survey papers without a worked
numerical example do not qualify. Synthetic matrices do not qualify.

## 3. Selection of k (portfolio size)

k = 3 for all instances. This matches the Fogo design and the paper's §5.

## 4. Objective groupings

Groups are taken directly from the criterion hierarchy published in the source
article (AHP tree, BWM criteria clusters, or thematic groupings stated by the
authors). At most 5 groups per instance; at least 2 criteria per group.

**Why one-criterion groups are excluded (Extension of Proposition 1):**
If each group contains exactly one criterion, then for any normalisation that
is affine per column relative to min-max (n_j = a_j·n_j^mm + b_j, a_j > 0),
the SAW score is an exact positive scalarisation of the group objectives.
Therefore Q2 = 1 by the Proposition 1 argument, for ANY affine normalisation.
Vector normalisation is affine; ranks is not. One-criterion groups would make
the vector cell trivially 100% and produce no signal. This is NOT a conservative
choice — it is a degenerate one that eliminates the measurement.

The three active instances use:
- milani2005: G1={bending,surface fatigue}, G2={UTS,surface hardness}, G3={core hardness}
- vafaei2018: G1={illumination,reachability}, G2={land texture}  [2 groups; n=3]
- junaid2024: G1={TS,E,FR,EB,Sf,OT,DU,RE}, G2={C}, G3={d,CO2,FP,WU,EU}

Note: vafaei2018 was originally assigned one group per criterion (degenerate).
This is corrected here; see DEV-005.

## 5. Minimum m

m ≥ 8. Instances with m < 8 remain in the corpus file for amplitude reporting
but are excluded from any Q2 analysis.

Excluded from Q2: bhaskar2023_asn (m=4), bhaskar2023_agr (m=4), chen2020 (m=5),

## 6. Experimental design

**Primary output (deterministic, no MC):**
For each instance and each normalisation (mm, vec, rnk): compute
TV, m_eff, ||delta||_2, TV_B, TV_W as defined in §7.

TV_B = between-group component of TV (distortion of group-level weights).
TV_W = within-group component (weighted mean of within-group TV).
See corpus_amplitudes.py for exact formulas and the Fogo contrafactual assertion.

**Secondary output (Monte Carlo, N=3500, κ=200):**
For each active instance: Q2 under mm/vec/rnk with objectives fixed in mm.
This requires coupling constraints to produce signal (see below). Reported
for completeness; not interpreted as evidence for H1 in the corpus alone.

**Why the corpus Q2 is uninformative without coupling constraints:**
Without a coupling constraint (e.g., spatial separation), greedy = optimal
by separability of the additive score. The frontier base rates in the three
active instances are 15.5%, 1.8% (two-group), and 34.5%. With these rates
and N=3500, Q2 is indistinguishable from 100% under any normalisation.

The instrument for the Q2 claim is the A_j(t) path in WS2, which has
coupling constraints and frontier base rate < 5%.

**Regression assertion (Proposition 1):**
For every instance × normalisation, the matched cell (score norm = objective
norm) must give Q2 = 100%. Hard assert in corpus_mc.py. Failure = bug.

**Scope of one-criterion groups (Extension of Prop. 1):**
For any affine normalisation (vector is affine; ranks is not), groups with
a single criterion give Q2 = 100% by the Proposition 1 argument. Reporting
these cells as "findings" would be a tautology. All active instances now use
multi-criterion groups.

## 8. Statistical reporting

For the amplitude table (primary):
- Report (TV, m_eff, ||delta||_2, TV_B, TV_W) per instance per normalisation.
- No confidence intervals needed (deterministic computation).

For the Q2 table (secondary, conditional on WS2):
- Report Q2 with binomial SE = sqrt(Q2*(100-Q2)/N).
- Do not report Q2 from the corpus alone as evidence for H1.
