ABSTRACT
In most of the manufacturing industries, the selection of optimal material among the ﬁnite
available alternatives becomes more diﬃcult with the more required performance criteria to
satisfy certain objectives. In such scenarios, Multi-Criteria-Decision-Making (MCDM) techniques,
such as hybrid AHP (Analytical hierarchy process) – TOPSIS (Technique for Order Preferences by
Similarity to Ideal Solution) and others assist in quantitative decision-making by accounting for
qualitative human judgments. The present work illustrates the application of a hybrid AHP-
TOPSIS technique to the ranking of alloy composites. The properties deﬁning data such as
physical, mechanical, thermal, thermo-mechanical, etc. are weighted using the AHP technique;
thereafter, relative weights are used to rank the alloy composite compositions by the TOPSIS
method. The compositions comprise the AA2024 alloy as the matrix phase and the reinforcing
phase consists of silicon carbide, silicon nitride and graphite particulates. The ranking analysis
orders are consistent with subjective analysis.
Dans la plupart des industries manufacturières, la sélection du matériau optimal parmi les choix
disponibles limités devient plus diﬃcile avec l’augmentation du nombre de critères de
performance requis pour satisfaire certains objectifs. Dans de tels scénarios, les techniques de
prise de décision multicritères (MCDM) telles que l’hybride AHP (processus de hiérarchie
analytique) – TOPSIS (technique de préférence de commande par similarité avec la solution
idéale) et d’autres aident à la prise de décision quantitative en tenant compte des jugements
humains qualitatifs. Le travail courant illustre l’application d’une technique hybride AHP –
TOPSIS au classement d’alliages composites. Les propriétés déﬁnissant les données telles que
physiques, mécaniques, thermiques, thermomécaniques, etc. sont pondérées à l’aide de la
technique AHP, par la suite, les poids relatifs sont utilisés pour classer les compositions
composites de l’alliage avec la méthode TOPSIS. Les compositions comprennent l’alliage
AA2024 comme phase de matrice et une phase de renforcement constituée de particules de
carbure de silicium, de nitrure de silicium et de graphite. Les ordres d’analyse du classement
s’avèrent cohérents avec l’analyse subjective.

1. Introduction
Metal matrix composites, especially aluminium metal
alloy composites reinforced with ceramic particulates
SiC, Al2O3, Si3N4, AlN, TiC, TiO2, etc, are the superior
candidates for the manufacturing of the diﬀerent automo-
tive and structural components such as gears, pistons,
cylinder liners, etc. owing to their light weight and high
strength to weight ratio [1]. Among the various grades
of wrought Al alloys, the AA2024 alloy comprising Cu
element provides a booster to its mechanical and tribolo-
gical characteristics and owing to that it is frequently
applied for the construction of components like drum
and rotor discs having a dry sliding wear phenomenon [2].
The addition of ceramic particulates in the Al alloy
matrix improved the ultimate tensile strength and
yield strength of the matrix alloy but, it may reduce its
ductility characteristic. The degree of improvement in
the properties depends upon the nature of the reinforce-
ment particulates and the fabrication method used
which could also aﬀect the interfacial bonding between
the two constituents of the composite [1]. To obtain the
positive eﬀects of the addition of ceramic particulates
while facing the above-mentioned diﬃculties simul-
taneously, a secondary reinforcement can be used for
the above-said applications. The addition of this sec-
ondary reinforcement could make a more ﬂexible and

The experimental work is ongoing continuously by
the material scholars for the improvement in the speciﬁc
characteristics such as physical, thermal, mechanical
and tribological properties of the above-picked alloy
through the incorporation of hybrid reinforcement.
Kok [4] examined the abrasive wear phenomenon of
Al2O3 particles reinforced AA2024 alloy composite and
observed that the wear resistance of aluminium matrix
composite was enhanced by incorporating alumina par-
ticles (10, 20, 30 wt.%). Zhiqiang et al. [5] evaluated the
improved wear performance in dry sliding conditions of
the Al-Cu-Mg alloy matrix reinforced with silicon par-
ticles (9 vol. fraction) with increasing magnitude of slid-
ing speed and normal load for base alloy as well as for
the composite.
Y. Sahin [6] found improved wear resistance under
various sliding conditions (reinforcement size, applied
load, abrasive grain size, and sliding distance) on the
wear behaviour of the AA2014 alloy composites having
10 wt.% SiC particulates of size (50 and 100 μm). Patel
et al. [7] observed superior abrasive wear resistance
characteristics of AA2024 alloy composites reinforced
with (9, 6 and 3 wt.%) SiC and (3, 6 and 9 wt.%)
ZrSiO4 particles. Zheng et al. [8] obtained improved
mechanical properties due to the grain reﬁnement of
the AA2024 alloy matrix having homogeneous dis-
persion of B4C particulates.
Bahrami et al. [9] evaluated the eﬀect of SiC particle
addition to enhance the mechanical characteristics of
the friction stir welded AA7075 alloy matrix. The ulti-
mate tensile strength, toughness and fatigue life of pre-
pared composites were improved due to the ﬁne grain
size of the Al matrix which consequently increased the
toughness of the composite. Miranda et al. [10] exam-
ined the damping properties of Al–Si-based MMCs
ﬁlled with 5 wt.% SiC particles and 2 wt.% CNT at
high temperature. A three-point bending position has
been used with a bending length of 40 mm. The damp-
ing capacity of the hybrid composite was enhanced with
the temperature for 1 and 50 Hz frequency but it
decreased with temperature for 100 Hz frequency. Lu
et al. [11] fabricated the AA7075 alloy hybrid composite
ﬁlled with 50 vol.% SiC particles and 5 vol.% Cr par-
ticles through the squeeze casting method and various
thermal characteristics have been investigated and com-
pared with AA7075/50 vol.% SiCp, AA7075/55 vol.%
SiCp. The thermal conductivity of the hybrid composite
was achieved more than a single-ﬁlled composite. A
lowered value of the coeﬃcient of thermal expansion
was obtained for AA7075/(50SiC + 5Cr) in comparison
to AA7075/50 vol.% SiC and AA7075/55 vol.% Cr.
Past work has shown various combinations of alu-
minium alloys reinforced with diﬀerent reinforcements,
especially ceramic particulates. The varying impact of
diﬀerent reinforcement particulates on the physical,
mechanical, thermal and tribological properties of the
prepared Al alloy composites has already been shown
by various authors, so it is diﬃcult to select any particu-
lar material reported in the above literature. Hence, the
selection of appropriate materials among diﬀerent
options on the basis of their performance is a tedious
task for a material scholar when there are so many
material properties involved. Hence this can be easily
done with the help of Multi-Criteria-Decision-Making
(MCDM) techniques [12]. The selection of material
for optimum product design also depends upon the fac-
tors such as performance (maximum), environmental
sensitivity and the minimum cost of the end product.
But some situations of conﬂicting nature are frequently
observed between the attributes/characteristics of the
materials and proposed objectives. In these kinds of
situations, it is required to identify the properties/attri-
butes having more impact or inﬂuence on the selection
of material for a speciﬁc engineering application than
others. Hence for the selection of appropriate material
best suited to the required component application,
many scholars of the related ﬁeld have studied and
applied numerous techniques of material selection
[13]. Jahan et al. [14] summarised diﬀerent Multi-Cri-
teria Decision Making (MCDM) techniques including
TOPSIS, AHP, fuzzy MCDM, ELECTRE, SAW, Goal
Programming, etc. for the evaluation of problems
associated
with
real-time
industrial
environment.
Some quantitative tools, such as chart method, materials
in product selection tools, cost per unit property
method, Neutral networks knowledge-based systems,
etc. of the initial screening for the material selection
are also reported. Ishizaka et al. [15] suggested a novel
technique known as the Group AHP Ordering Method
for the new production facilities selection.
Xuebin [16] applied a technique known as Non-
dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm (NSGA-II) for
evaluating Pareto sets. TOPSIS technique was also
used with entropy weights to select the optimum sol-
ution for environmental and economic power dispatch

Hence from the above discussion, it has been
observed that very limited works have been done for
the optimal selection of AA2024 hybrid alloy composite
formulations using the hybrid AHP-TOPSIS method-
ology. The work elaborated here mainly focused on
synthesising the hybrid composite formulations con-
taining the AA2024 alloy as a matrix and diﬀerent
amounts of micro-size ceramic particulates like SiC
and Si3N4 along with graphite micro-size particulates
as secondary reinforcement. The various properties,
such as physical, mechanical and tribological of the pre-
pared metal alloy composites, were evaluated through
experimentation followed by the determination of the
performance ranking order using the hybrid AHP-
TOPSIS methodology under diﬀerent performance
determining criteria of conﬂicting nature.

2.3. Ranking optimisation using hybrid AHP-
TOPSIS
The hybrid AHP-TOPSIS approach could be used to
determine the optimal composite composition among
other prepared composites that overall optimises the
required criteria of multi-criteria and multi-alternative
nature i.e. Multi-Criteria-Decision-Making (MCDM)
methods
like
hybrid
AHP-TOPSIS
[39].
Such
decision-making tools aid in eﬀective decision-making
whenever the criteria are conﬂicting [40]. In the present
work, the hybrid AHP-TOPSIS technique is used to ﬁnd
out the ranking of composites prepared under diﬀerent
evaluated performance-deﬁning criteria. Such decision-
making tools are frequently applied in many areas such
as the military, management, society, etc. and obtaining
more and more attention [31].
The AHP was proposed by Thomas L. Saaty [22–
25,41] around the 1970s. It provides relative prioritisa-
tion of criteria to decide. The basic steps involved are
the identiﬁcation of PDCs, material alternatives and
decision goals. After that, all the collected information
is represented in the form of a hierarchy chart which
needs priority examination and assurance of relative
weights with consistency veriﬁcation. The description
of the algorithm is as follows [42]:
Step 1: PDCs determination and preparation of hierar-
chy structure:
Here, the obtained results of diﬀerent characteristics
like physical, mechanical, etc. are used as performance-
deﬁning criteria, as shown in Table 3 [31]. Also, the
obtained information is represented in a hierarchy
structure, as shown in Figure 4.
Step 2: Pair-wise comparison matrix generation and
computation of relative weight:
The pair-wise comparison matrix is generated on the
basis of human judgement and Sattay’s 1–9 scale, as

3.1. Magnitude evaluation of the performance
determining criterion for ASN alloy composites
The characteristics of alloy composites are dependent
upon the type/content of matrix and reinforcement,
the distribution of reinforcement into the matrix and
the strength of interface thus developed between
matrix-reinforcement. All these depend upon careful
design and fabrication procedures.
3.1.1. Physical characterisation of ASN alloy
composites
Table 6 shows the calculation of void content, actual or
experimental density evaluated through Archimedes
principle and theoretical density via the rule of mixture.
There is a diﬀerence between both densities because
of some void content that is always present as a result of
insuﬃcient wettability between particulates and matrix.
But void content is reduced as the weight content of
Si3N4 particulates increased from 0 to 6 wt.%, The
void content shows a diminishing trend with the con-
tent of the harder phases such as SiC and Si3N4; there-
fore, it seems that the presence of SiC and Si3N4
phases in the matrix-enhanced interfacial bonding
between the constituents of the composite, consequently
void content reduces. The increment in the experimen-
tal density from 2.690 g/cc for ASN-0 to 2.750 g/cc for
ASN-6 of the prepared composites was due to the higher
density of the Si3N4 particulates (3.31 g/cc) than that of
the AA2024 (2.78 g/cc). The occurrence of impurities in
the constituents of the composite (matrix and ﬁller
particulates) might also be the reason for the presence
of a small amount of porosity [44].

From the above analysis of the mechanical properties
of prepared composites, it has been obtained that the
addition of Si3N4 ceramic particulates improves the var-
ious mechanical properties of the prepared composites
but, a partial eﬀect of solid lubricant (graphite) provides
a balance between the strength and hardness of the com-
posite and makes the material suitable for machining
purpose. Diﬀerent factors are responsible for the
improvement in mechanical properties. These factors
include the nature of reinforced particulates, the distri-
bution of particulates in the matrix and the interface
formed between the matrix and reinforcement. Strain
ﬁelds are generated due to the thermal mismatch between
the AA2024 alloy and Si3N4 particulates because there is a
diﬀerence in the coeﬃcients of thermal expansion of two
constituents (matrix and reinforcement). The motion of
dislocations gets restricted through the strain ﬁelds gen-
erated and Si3N4 particulates are distributed uniformly in
the aluminium matrix. The Orowan strengthening mech-
anism has played an important role by which Si3N4 par-
ticulates are homogeneously scattered in the matrix,
impeding the motion of dislocations [45]. One more
reason for improvement in the mechanical characteristics
was the grain size reduction of the aluminium matrix due
to the uniform dispersion of ﬁne-size SiC particles
according to the Hall-Petch relationship. Hence, grain
size reduction provides the path of dislocation to be
changed so that the interface can eﬀectively transfer the
load and hard particles can eﬀectively restrict the crack-
ing and deformation. The impact of the above factors
was enhanced as the content of hard ceramic particles
increased which consequently enhanced the hardness
and strength of the fabricated composite materials.

3.1.4. Evaluation of thermal, thermo-mechanical
and wear characterisation of ASN composites
The analysis was also done to evaluate the thermal and
thermo-mechanical characteristics of the prepared alloy
composites and the following results were obtained. The
thermal conductivity reduced from 178 W/mK to
169 W/mK, a ∼5% decrease with the addition of
2 wt.% Si3N4 hard ceramic particulates. Furthermore,
it reduced by ∼5% to 160 W/mK with the addition of
4 wt.% Si3N4 hard ceramic particulates. It further
reduced by ∼10% to 144 W/mK with the addition of
6 wt.% Si3N4 hard ceramic phases. Hence, more weight
content of the Si3N4 phase in the alloy composites
enhances the thermal barrier conductance, as suggested
by Kawai et al. [48]. There is a slight enhancement in the
material stability with the increasing amount of Si3N4
particulates for the temperature, so the overall material
stability order is ASN-6 > ASN-4 > ASN-2 > ASN-0 with
the applicable temperature range. The alloy composite
with ASN-6 composition has shown more than 100%
material stability from room temperature of 25°C to
596°C maximum temperature of the experiment.
The storage modulus (E′) of the prepared alloy com-
posite shows a diminishing trend with the entire temp-
erature range. The reason for enhancement in storage
modulus with the amount of Si3N4 particulates is the
improvement in the mechanical characteristics of the
alloy composites as discussed earlier. At a particular
temperature, the order of E′ was: ASN-6 > ASN-4 >
ASN-2 > ASN-0. Also, the uniform dispersion of the
particulate phase in the matrix improves the damping
vibration capacity of alloy composites [49]. The loss
modulus (E′′) of the alloy composite increased with
the entire temperature range. The order of E′′ is
observed as ASN-0 > ASN-2 > ASN-4 > ASN-6, in the
temperature range of 25–250°C. Tan δ or damping
capacity of the alloy composite is increased for the entire
temperature range irrespective of the composition but at
a particular temperature, the order is as follows ASN-6
> ASN-4 > ASN-2 > ASN-0. It follows that damping
capacity is enhanced with Si3N4 particulates as the dis-
sipation rate of energy increases with an increasing
amount of Si3N4 particulates which leads to an increase
in the mechanical characteristics when composites are
subjected to external load conditions [50].
The speciﬁc wear rate of ASN alloy composites is
reduced with respect to all the controllable factors
except temperature. The coeﬃcient of friction is
decreased with normal load and sliding velocity but
the magnitude of the coeﬃcient of friction is increased
with respect to sliding distance and environment temp-
erature. In terms of the amount of the Si3N4 particulates,
the order of speciﬁc wear rate and coeﬃcient of friction
at a particular level of all the respective factors is : ASN-
0 > ASN-2 > ASN-4 > ASN-6. It means the speciﬁc wear
rate and coeﬃcient of friction have a decreasing trend
with the increasing amount of Si3N4 particulates. A
detailed analysis of speciﬁc wear rate and coeﬃcient of
friction of ASN alloy composites has been reported by
Bhaskar et al. [51].

3.2. Magnitude evaluation of performance
determining criterion for AGR alloy composites
Both the physical characteristics, such as experimental
density and void content of the prepared alloy compo-
sites, are described in Table 9.
3.2.1. Physical characterisation of AGR alloy
composites
Both the theoretical and experimental density of the
AGR composites decreased with the weight content of
graphite particulates, as shown in Figure 8. This may
be due to the lower density of the graphite particulates
(2.28 g/cc) compared to the SiC (3.21 g/cc), Si3N4
(3.31 g/cc) particulates and the AA2024 alloy matrix
(2.78 g/cc). Hence, the incorporation of a higher num-
ber of graphite particulates in the alloy matrix reduces
the overall actual density of the composite. Similar
results were obtained by Ravindran et al. [53].
3.2.2. Mechanical characterisation of AGR alloy
composites
The impact of graphite particulate addition in the
AA2024 alloy on the mechanical characteristics of the
prepared composites is shown in Table 10. The magni-
tudes of micro-hardness, ﬂexural and impact strength of
the AGR alloy composites are signiﬁcantly decreased
with the weight content of Gr particulates, as shown
in Figure 9. The maximum value of micro-hardness is
108 HV for the AGR-0 composite. The minimum
value of micro-hardness is 83 HV corresponding to
AGR-6 composition. The maximum value of ﬂexural
strength is obtained at 339.71 MPa for the AGR-0 com-
posite, whereas the minimum magnitude of ﬂexural
strength is 239.67 MPa corresponding to the AGR-6
composite. The maximum impact energy is 81.7 J for
the AGR-0 composite and the minimum impact energy
is 33.5 J for the AGR-6 composition. The ultimate ten-
sile strength (UTS) of fabricated AGR alloy composite
is decreased as the graphite particulate content is
increased. The maximum value of UTS is215.14 MPa
for the AGR-0 and the minimum magnitude of UTS
is 134.43 MPa corresponding to AGR-6 composition,
as shown in Figure 10. The percentage elongation is
decreased as the weight content of graphite particulates
increases from 0 to 6 wt.%. The maximum elongation
(11.63%) is achieved for the AGR-0 composite i.e. with-
out the presence of graphite particulates and for the
AGR-6 composite; the elongation is reduced to 2.67%.
3.2.3. Fracture toughness of AGR composites
The fracture toughness of AGR alloy composites is eval-
uated as stress intensity factor (K) at diﬀerent crack
lengths of 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5 mm, as shown in Table 11
and Figure 11. For diﬀerent crack lengths, the order of
‘K’ is AGR-0 > AGR-2 > AGR-4 > AGR-6. The fracture
toughness for a particular crack length is reduced as
the weight content of graphite particulates increases
from 0 to 6 wt.%. The lowered magnitude of the stress
intensity factor is due to the eﬀect of the higher brittle-
ness of graphite particulates compared to the ductile Al
alloy matrix. The lower density of graphite particulates
compared to the Al alloy allowed it to be suspended in
the melt and non-uniform dispersion of graphite parti-
culates is observed that further enhanced with the
higher amount of addition of graphite particulates.
Hence the combined eﬀect of lower density and brittle-
ness decreased the physical and mechanical character-
istics of alloy composites [3].

and wear characterisation of AGR composites
Thermal and thermo-mechanical properties of AGR

trend with the increasing amount of Gr particulates,

144.28 W/mK, ∼10% decrease with the addition of

3.4. Ranking optimisation of alloy composites
using the TOPSIS algorithm
The Technique for Order Preferences by Similarity to
Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) algorithm is powerful to rank
the material alternatives. The TOPSIS is an excellent
approach among various MCDM techniques to select
the desired material based on various performance

criteria as this technique allows the user to explore sev-
eral alternatives (materials) and attributes (material
characteristics) for material selection. The response of
this technique is based on the ranking methodology
and the ranking selection is based upon the quantitative
magnitude (weighting criteria) of each alternative that
could be deﬁned by the engineer or analyst. The idea
of the best alternative is based on the fact that the
selected alternative or material should possess the mini-
mum distance from the positive ideal solution and the
maximum distance from the negative ideal solution
[49]. It begins with normalising the magnitudes of
values correspondence to PDCs to omit the diﬀerences
in their measuring units for relative comparison. Sub-
sequently, applying the weights achieved from the
AHP technique, the weighted normalised decision
matrix can be generated. After that, the positive ideal
solution and negative ideal solution are evaluated e.g.
if the PDCs are beneﬁt criteria such as tensile strength,
then the maximum value would be ideal; however, if the
criteria are cost or non-beneﬁt criteria like speciﬁc wear
rate, then the minimum value would be ideal. There-
after, Euclidian distance is measured and relative close-
ness to the ideal solution is computed. Alternatives,
having higher closeness are ranked ﬁrst relative to
others.
